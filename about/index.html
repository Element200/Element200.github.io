I'm a graduate student at Yale University. I just finished my master's degree at Simon Fraser University. I tend to work at the interface of biology and physics, with a strong focus on the physics. (I once had a professor who said that there's biophysics and biophysics, and I consider myself a biophysicist.) In fact, three of my four big research projects, although with biophysicists, involved no biology whatsoever; if anything, I worked very hard to ensure that there was no biology in my samples.

I've loved physics for pretty much as long as I could remember. I don't know why I like it; there's just something about how pretty it all is once it comes together. I don't know that it's really about how well it explains the universe; I tend to regard that as somewhat neat but don't care that much about it. I think I've reached a stage in my life where I think it's just poetic, y'know? Like, the Navier-Stokes equations are extremely useful in allowing us to design aeroplanes and such, but I think it's just awesome that such a simple equation can have such emergent complexity baked into it. And it goes so much further! All the little modifications you can make to the Navier-Stokes equation to make it describe compressible things or relativistic things or whatever -- all of these are things I find wonderful. I'm particularly fond of all the little ah-ha moments physics can give you when you're working through something extremely mathematical.
